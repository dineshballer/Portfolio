The honest verdict

You are one credibility pass away from a top tier portfolio.

The work is not the problem. Your case studies are structured better than most senior portfolios I benchmark against: snapshots, quick facts, insight led headings, real decisions, post launch sections, even "patterns the next team reused." That is a lead level signal. The thing holding you back is not depth, it is trust friction and craft sprawl: a name that changes between pages, LinkedIn links that point at three different places, a home page that repeats your About page, two near identical Dell AI case studies, a buzzword you told yourself to never use, and a type and color system that is 80% disciplined and 20% loose.

None of it is hard to fix. Almost all of it is copy, links, ordering, and tightening. A recruiter who scans for ninety seconds should leave certain of three things: who you are, that the work shipped and moved numbers, and how to reach you. Right now the first and third wobble. Fix those and the case studies, which are your real asset, finally get read.

How I judged this

The bar I held you to, and how I looked.

The benchmark

Two audiences, two bars. Senior IC hiring managers spend roughly two minutes on a first pass and scan for one thing: "can this person solve a problem like mine." They want intentional decisions, business impact, and taste in what you chose to show. The big tech / FAANG bar adds a craft tax on top: a tight type system, deliberate motion, fast loads, airtight accessibility, and zero rough edges, because the portfolio itself is read as a work sample.

I also held you to the standard set by the reference designers in your own formula doc (Hanson Wu, Jessica Hsu, Sanvi Thithi for structure; Brittany Chiang and Rauno Freiberg for craft and micro interactions; Brian Lovin for the clean senior "Now" structure) and to Aneta Kmiecik's research across 500+ portfolios that informs your playbook.

How I looked

I assessed your local working files (the newest versions, edited within the last day), not the live site. I rendered all seven pages headlessly at desktop (1440px) and mobile (390px) to capture the screenshots embedded throughout, then analyzed the underlying code: document structure and headings, every link target, the full color palette with computed WCAG contrast, the complete type scale, the motion budget, image and alt inventory, and meta tags.

Every number in this report is measured from your files: hex values, contrast ratios, font sizes, link counts, heading trees. Where I make a visual judgment, the screenshot is right there so you can check me.

Page 1 · The first impression

Home: distinctive and warm, but it makes a recruiter work.

Your home page has a real point of view: the chip lens hero, the Folio AI chat, the editorial type. That is rare and good. The problem is everything it asks the visitor to do before the work appears, and the credibility leaks hiding in the chrome.

Strength The hero has a genuine identity

Fraunces + General Sans + a Caveat accent, warm ivory, the chip lens mechanic, and an embedded AI chat. This does not look like a Framer template. Against the "don't look like everyone else" test, you pass. Keep this.

High The work sits too low

Above the fold is name, chip lens, and the Folio prompt. Strong case study teasers with real metrics exist, but a two minute scanner meets your personality before your proof. Pull one shipped, metric heavy case higher, or add a single line of hard proof near the hero.

Fix: One sentence of static positioning + one metric within the first viewport, then the work.
High "AI-powered" is in your own meta

Your meta description reads "building B2B products and AI-powered workflows." Your house rules explicitly ban "AI-powered." A recruiter who shares your link sees this exact line in the preview.

Fix: Rewrite the meta description and OG copy in your real voice. No "AI-powered."
Medium Em dashes in the card teasers

The Loan Deboarding teaser still uses an em dash ("from a single record — replacing parallel spreadsheets"). Small, but it is your one hard typographic rule, and you broke it on the most visible page.

Fix: Sweep visible copy for "—" and replace with a comma, period, or colon.

Card ordering and labels

Home features four cards in this order: 01 Loan Deboarding (Onity), 02 Real-Time Inquiry (Onity), 03 IntelliAssist 2.0 (Dell, "Shipped · 2024"), 04 Dell INC (Dell, "Shipped · 2022"). Two issues:

  • Your two lead cards carry a "Designs modified · NDA" eyebrow instead of a recency or outcome signal, while your shipped AI work with the strongest, most 2026 relevant numbers (55% tickets cut, 92% self serve) sits third. For both the recruiter scan and big tech AI relevance, that is your strongest foot, and it is not forward.
  • The fifth case study (the original IntelliAssist GEN-AI chat) is not featured here at all, yet it is still live and linked from every case study footer. More on that in the case studies section.

Mobile

The mobile render holds together, which many editorial portfolios fail at. Watch the hero stack height: on a phone the visitor scrolls noticeably before reaching the first card. The same "lift the work" fix helps here twice as much.

Page 2 · The human

About: warm and well stocked, but it overlaps Home and opens soft.

This is where the person shows up, and you have the right ingredients: a journey, a beliefs section, recognition, testimonials, a "beyond the screen" gallery with summits and a graduation. The issues are duplication, a soft opener, and a couple of dated signals.

High Home and About repeat four sections

"Four words, one operating manual" (Curious / Calm / Specific / Useful), the awards strip, the tools row, and the experience timeline all appear on both pages. That makes Home long and strips About of a reason to exist.

Fix: Home keeps pitch, work, and a light contact line. About owns the story, career timeline, recognition, and testimonials. Each section lives in exactly one place.
Medium The opener is poetic, not positioning

The current h1, "Curious by nature and a designer by choice, crafting interactions that bridge logic with emotion," is lovely but could belong to any designer. For a senior IC it should locate you: who you design for and what you make.

Fix: Open with something like your "designing calm into complex systems / for the analysts, AMs, and ops teams who live inside the tools" line, then get poetic.
Medium Adobe XD is in your toolkit

The tools row features Adobe XD, which Adobe discontinued. To a hiring manager that reads as a stack that has not been refreshed. (The XD purple also leaks into the page's color set.)

Fix: Show the current stack: Figma, FigJam, and the research / handoff tools you actually use now.
Low Missing meta description

About has no meta description, so its link preview will be auto scraped. Your case studies all have good ones; About is the gap.

Pages 3 to 7 · Your real asset

Case studies: structurally, these are senior to lead level.

This is the strongest part of your portfolio and you should know it. Before the critiques, here is what you are doing that most portfolios, including many at big tech, do not.

Strength The architecture hits nearly every best practice

Across all five, you consistently ship: a sticky in page section nav (Overview, Snapshot, Problem, Solution, Final, Validation, Post launch, Retrospective), a "The short version" snapshot, a Quick Facts block (Role, Team, Timeline, Tools), an NDA confidentiality note, and most importantly insight led headings instead of method labels. "Lost in the back-and-forth," "The chat box was tiny. The job it had to do wasn't," "What's missing isn't visibility, it's structure." That is exactly how Aneta Kmiecik and the senior reference set say to write headings.

Strength You show real decisions

The MVP scoping call out, "Some reps wanted the panel back. Here's why I held the line," and "It was a harder build, and I had to make that case to engineering." Defending what did not get built is the clearest seniority signal there is. Keep doing this in every case.

Strength Process impact, and great alt text

Each case ends with "patterns the next team reused," which is a lead level move most designers skip. And your case study images carry rich, descriptive alt text, good for both accessibility and SEO. The home and about pages do not match this standard yet.

It reads as padding, and it dilutes your strongest, most current story. It also creates a metric collision: "85" appears as both an SUS score and an adoption percentage, and "92" is "task completion" in one case and "self serve adoption" in the other.

Three ways out, in order of preference

1. Merge into one evolution story. "I shipped IntelliAssist, then rebuilt it." v1 becomes the first act, 2.0 the payoff. This is the strongest narrative and removes the redundancy.
2. Keep both but differentiate hard. Frame v1 as the 0 to 1 research and launch, 2.0 as the scale and redesign, and remove v1 from the "more work" footers so it is reachable only inside the story.
3. Cut v1. If 2.0 already tells the whole arc, retire v1.

And: lock every metric to one definition. Decide what "85" and "92" each mean and use that wording everywhere.

Cross cutting · The FAANG craft tax

Craft: the system is 80% disciplined. Tighten the other 20%.

Your core palette and type pairing are genuinely good. At the big tech bar, the tells are in the scale, the accent colors, and the motion budget.

High The type scale sprawls to ~30 sizes

Home alone uses roughly eighteen discrete pixel sizes (9, 10, 11, 11.5, 12, 12.5, 13, 13.5, 14, 14.5, 15, 16, 17, 18, 20, 22, 28, 36) plus eleven clamp() values. A senior type system is a deliberate ladder of six to nine steps. Several sizes (9 to 11px) are cramped microcopy below comfortable reading size.

Fix: Define a token scale (for example 12, 14, 16, 18, 22, 28, 38, 52) and map everything to it. Raise minimums to 12 to 13px.
Medium Two accent colors fail AA for normal text

Your core palette passes comfortably (see the data section). But the terracotta accent #c75b39 on ivory is 3.75:1 and the grey #888 is 3.15:1, both below the 4.5:1 needed for normal text. If they appear on small labels or microcopy, that is an accessibility miss and, at the FAANG bar, a craft miss.

Fix: Darken the terracotta and the grey for any text use, or reserve them for large text and non text UI only.
Medium The motion budget is high

Durations range from 0.15s to 5s with staggered entry delays up to ~1.7s and 21 timed sequences on Home. The principle is "one orchestrated moment, then calm." You are closer to several moments. Trim ambient motion and bring total entry under ~600ms so the pitch lands fast.

Credit: you do honor prefers-reduced-motion, which many portfolios ignore.

Strength The identity is distinctive

Fraunces display + General Sans body + Caveat accent, on warm ivory with a deep forest accent, is a real, ownable look. Do not flatten it while tightening. The goal is discipline within the voice, not a different voice.

Cross cutting · Accessibility

Accessibility: strong inside case studies, leaky on Home and About.

Strength Case study alt text is excellent

Every case study image carries specific, descriptive alt text. That is rare and it helps SEO too. prefers-reduced-motion is also handled.

Medium Empty alt on Home and About

Home has an image with empty alt; About has the Figma and Adobe XD tool logos with empty alt. Small, but it breaks the standard your case studies set.

Medium Contrast misses

See Craft: terracotta and the light greys fail AA for body text. Run a contrast pass on every label and caption color.

Low Verify focus states and tab order

The chip lens, the Folio chat, and the sticky case nav are all interactive. Confirm visible focus rings and a logical keyboard path through each. (This needs a live keyboard pass, which is the one thing a code read cannot fully confirm.)

Cross cutting · Performance

Performance: the quietest thing hurting your first impression.

High No lazy loading anywhere

Not one image uses loading="lazy". Your case studies carry 12 to 16 images each. Every one loads upfront, which slows the page a recruiter is deciding about in the first seconds.

Fix: Add loading="lazy" to all below the fold images. Consider WebP and a few short muted loop videos for interaction heavy screens.
Medium No width or height on images

Images have no intrinsic dimensions set, which causes layout shift (CLS) as they load. That is a measurable ding on the same Lighthouse a big tech reviewer might run.

Fix: Set width and height (or aspect-ratio) on every image so the layout reserves space.

None of this is visible to you on a fast connection with a warm cache. It is very visible to a recruiter on hotel wifi opening you for the first time.
