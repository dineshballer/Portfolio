Critical

The 62% has a provenance problem, and the page admits it

The Inquiry portal is labeled "In development · 2026" and its validation section says the numbers come from prototype testing. But the home card says it is "cutting case resolution by 62%", the case hero says the emails "stopped" and managers "got six hours back" (past tense), and the Key Decision section says "the 62% resolution improvement validated the call." A product that has not launched cannot have improved resolution by 62%. One interview question ("how was that measured?") collapses it, and May's audit already flagged the tense. The fix is not deleting the number; it is labeling it: "projected 62%, modeled from prototype testing against the current email baseline." Honest labels are explicitly rewarded; claimed-but-unmeasured numbers are the single fastest trust killer in the 2026 research.
Fix: one canonical metrics table (in a doc, not on the site) with value, definition, status (measured / projected), source, and tense. Then sweep: home cards, case heroes, snapshots, validation blocks, Sage's system prompt, resume bullets. Every number appears with one value, one unit, one status, everywhere.
