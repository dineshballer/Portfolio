Simpler case study titles and one shared template

A review of all five case studies against Aneta Kmiecik's course and what other product design leads recommend in 2026. Built to make the work scan in the two minutes a hiring manager actually spends.

Nothing here is live. This is the suggest step. Tell me which title direction you like and I will change it in prod, one file at a time.

1 · What I found

Your on-page hero headlines are already tight and clear. The problems sit one layer up, on the homepage cards, and in how the template is built underneath.

Card titles run long

The homepage cards have grown into multi-clause sentences. The Inquiry Portal card is 35 words with no period. A recruiter scanning at speed cannot hold that.

Card and hero drifted apart

Card says "I built", hero says "I designed". Card says "inquiry visibility", hero says "case visibility". Small tells, but a senior eye catches them.

Two different templates

The three Onity studies use Snapshot / Problem area. The two Dell studies use The Challenge / The Solution. The set does not feel like one person's system.

Heavy to read

Each study runs 4,600 to 6,100 words across 8 to 16 sections. Your north star (bevyip) lands the same job in roughly 600 words of body.

Naming bug to fix regardless of titles

The Dell INC study calls itself "Intelligence Nerve Center" four times, while the homepage also calls it "information network center." Pick one true expansion of INC and use it everywhere. Inconsistent product names read as carelessness.

2 · What Aneta and other leads say

  • Aneta Kmiecik (your course notes): title is first-person and names a specific outcome. Section headings should be the insight, not the method. "User research" is weak; "Analysts did not trust the dashboard because the numbers disagreed" is strong. Every case study needs a 3-line snapshot up top: problem, change, results.
  • Aneta, 2026: frame each study as a short story, lead with insight and impact over method. Portfolios now win on craft and first impression, not process depth.
  • uxfol.io and UX Playbook (2026 guides): scannable beats thorough. Short sections, strong headings, conclusion first (the pyramid principle). If your value is not clear in 30 seconds, the longer read never happens.
  • Peer title formulas: "Improving [outcome] through [design change]" and "Boosted conversion 15% through a streamlined checkout for 50k shoppers" beat "Design checkout process." The title is the first promise you make. A vague one makes strong work forgettable.

The throughline: keep your first-person voice (it is your differentiator and Aneta endorses it), but enforce economy. One idea, one number, one period.

3 · One simpler template for all five

Collapse 8 to 16 sections down to seven blocks, each carrying one idea. Apply the same skeleton to every study so they read as a set. Target roughly 800 words of body, down from 5,000.

Now (varies per study)

Snapshot
Problem area / The Challenge
The call I'd defend / Operating model
Solution, in many parts
Multiple screen walk-throughs
Tracked / validation blocks
Retrospective
8 to 16 labeled sections, 5,000+ words

Proposed (same for all five)

1 · Hero Product line, first-person headline, 2 metrics, one cover image
2 · Snapshot Three lines: problem, change, result
3 · Quick facts Role, team, timeline, what was mine
4 · The problem Insight as the heading, one annotated "before"
5 · What I designed Two or three decisions, each a heading + why + one image
6 · The call I'd defend One ownership moment: what you decided or said no to
7 · Results + what's next Metrics, then a short honest retrospective

Block 6 is the AI-era flex Aneta keeps pushing: since your screens are NDA recreations, the decisions are what is provably yours. Keep one per study, prominent.

Option B splits it into a plain-English label plus a first-person line, which is the easiest to scan. Pick one style and I will apply it across the set so they stay consistent.
