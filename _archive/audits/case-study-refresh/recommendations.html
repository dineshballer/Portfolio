Lean in
Why bevyip feels so clean

It is not the font. It is one idea per screen.

Beverly's Confido case study is maybe 600 words of body copy total. Every section is the same four-part move, with a lot of air around it. That is the whole trick, and it is copyable.

01

Small label

CONTEXT, PROBLEM #1, SOLUTION #2. A quiet uppercase tag, nothing more.

02

A headline that is the point

"Approval panels were buried 3 levels deep." The sentence carries the idea, not a clever abstraction.

03

One clarifying line

A single sentence underneath, often with the number baked in. No "Why:" preamble.

04

One big image, lots of air

A full-width screen, then whitespace, then the next idea fades up. You never read a paragraph.

The "sheet that comes up" reveal

How bevyip gets that slow, rising feel.

It is two things working together. A smooth-scroll layer that slows the whole page down, and a reveal that lifts each block up as it enters. Here is the recipe, ready to drop into your case studies when you want it.

  1. Smooth scroll (Lenis). bevyip runs the Lenis library, which interpolates the scroll position. That alone is most of the "slow sheet" feel: the page glides instead of jumping. One script tag and three lines to init.
  2. Rise + fade on enter. Each section starts 28 to 40px low and transparent, then an IntersectionObserver adds a class that animates it up to place. Slow ease-out, around 800ms.
  3. Stagger. Children lift a beat after each other (60 to 90ms apart) so a block unfolds rather than popping.
  4. Optional, the literal sheet. Wrap a headline in a clipped box and slide the text up from 100%, so it looks like paper being raised. Use sparingly, on section titles.
